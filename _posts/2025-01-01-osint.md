Open Source Intelligence (OSINT) involves collecting, analyzing, and synthesizing publicly available information to generate actionable intelligence. Whether utilized in adversary emulation, digital footprinting, corporate threat intelligence, or law enforcement investigations, OSINT provides crucial insights into attack surfaces and threat actor behaviors without directly interacting with target systems.

This guide details the core phases, investigation categories, tools, and operational security (OPSEC) guidelines for OSINT research.

### 1. Domain & Infrastructure OSINT
* **DNS & Passive Historical Data**: Certificate Transparency (CT) logs, WHOIS history, passive DNS databases (SecurityTrails, Censys, Shodan).
* **IP & ASN Mapping**: Subnet ranges, hosting providers, reverse IP lookups, BGP routing tables.

### 2. Digital Footprinting & Identity Investigation
* **Social Media Intelligence (SOCMINT)**: Username correlation, email reverse lookups, metadata extraction (Exif data), archived webpage analysis (Wayback Machine).
* **Breached Data Analysis**: Identifying leaked credentials, compromised domain emails, and exposure risks without unauthorized access.

### 3. Code Repositories & Cloud Exposures
* **Source Code Intelligence**: Searching GitHub, GitLab, and Paste sites for leaked API keys, hardcoded credentials, internal domain names, and git commit history.
* **Public Cloud Asset Discovery**: Enumerating exposed S3 buckets, Azure blobs, and GCP storage containers.

---

## OPSEC Rules for OSINT Analysts

1. **Sock Puppets & Burner Identities**: Maintaining compartmentalized research personas with dedicated virtual environments.
2. **Traffic Isolation**: Routing investigative traffic through VPNs, Tor, or isolated proxies to prevent reverse-reconnaissance.
3. **Data Integrity & Verification**: Cross-referencing findings across multiple independent sources to avoid false attribution.
